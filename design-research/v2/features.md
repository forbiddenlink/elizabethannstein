## Gaps nobody fills

1. **Verifiable proof.** Every site claims (counters, client names, "100k+ installs") and none lets the visitor check. A ledger of live systems with real status and a checked-at time is unoccupied, and it is the current homepage's strongest asset.
2. **Hiring-ready in one glance.** Only Delba states availability and only Brittany links a resume. No site pairs availability, resume, and a one-click contact in a single above-the-fold block.
3. **Case studies with a fixed structure.** No extraction reported a repeatable case study template (problem, constraints, stack, outcome, what broke). The spec-sheet spread can be the first.
4. **Failure and maintenance history.** Nobody shows incidents, uptime history or "last maintained". Arc'teryx-style repair claims (see references.md #10) exist outside the category only.
5. **Roles in one person.** Developer and designer are shown as one blended role everywhere. Nobody tags each project by what was designed versus engineered versus operated.
6. **Provenance of the build.** None name the host, database, and vendors per project (see references.md #9, bakery supplier model).
7. **Scannable for recruiters and rich for engineers.** The finalists split: teaching sites (Josh, Lee) have no work, demo sites (Maxime, Bruno) have no recruiter path. A dense list view plus a plate view (references.md #14) serves both.
8. **Broken-state honesty.** Bruno's offline leaderboard shows the risk of live elements. No site designs the "service is down right now" state deliberately. A ledger that shows a red row with a timestamp would turn the risk into the point.
